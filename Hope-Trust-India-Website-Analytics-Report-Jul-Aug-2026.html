Executive summary

Over the 30-day period the website recorded 76,538 pageviews from 28,052 unique visitors, averaging roughly 2,551 pageviews and 935 visitors per day, with traffic holding a stable baseline of 2,000–2,600 pageviews daily and no availability-related dips.

On Google Search, the site earned 1,900 clicks from 163,000 impressions — clicks grew 3% versus the previous period while impressions fell 16%, meaning the site is converting a smaller pool of search appearances more efficiently. Average CTR is 1.2% and average position is 12.3, which places most rankings at the bottom of page one or the top of page two.

Two structural findings shape this report. First, search demand is overwhelmingly brand-led — around 92% of clicks from the top queries come from people already searching for "Hope Trust" by name, while non-branded discovery terms such as "therapist near me" and "psychologist hyderabad" generate high impressions but convert at under 1%. Second, the geographic profile of raw pageviews and of verified search visitors differ sharply, which indicates a significant share of recorded pageviews is automated rather than human. Both are addressed in Key Findings.

Traffic pattern over the month

Daily pageviews opened the period at roughly 2,400, spiked to a period high of about 4,200 on 12 July, then settled into a consistent 1,900–2,600 band for the remainder of the month. Three secondary peaks near 3,000 occurred around 2, 5 and 8 August, suggesting content or referral activity in the first week of August. Unique visitors followed the same shape at roughly 1,100–1,300 per day, with peaks near 2,000 on 11 and 15 July and 3 August. The final day, 9 August, is a partial day and reads lower for that reason only.

The consistency of the baseline is the notable point: there is no erosion trend across the month, and the site served every request without a measurable availability gap.

Content performance — what the page data says

Working well

  • The homepage is doing its job at 16.5% of all pageviews — brand searchers land there first.
  • /book-your-session/ is the third most-viewed page (3,201 views) and its search clicks are up 19%. This is the commercial page and it is trending in the right direction.
  • /mental-health/ (1,914) and /contact/ (1,237) both rank in the top seven, showing visitors move from content into service and enquiry pages.
  • The addiction blog cluster — gambling addiction, spiritual experience, alcoholism, Zen and recovery, malnutrition — draws around 4,500 views and aligns closely with the core service offering.

Needs attention

  • The largest blog post is off-topic. "9 Interesting Traits of People Who Never Post on Social Media" (3,372 views) outperforms every service page but has no direct relationship to treatment services.
  • Service pages sit low. /addiction/ (568) and /training/ (512) each attract under 1% of traffic despite representing core revenue lines.
  • The blog index (1,439) outranks most individual service pages, indicating visitors browse content rather than being routed toward services.
  • No conversion measurement is in place — pageviews are known, but enquiries, form submissions and bookings are not tracked against them.

Key findings

1. A large share of recorded pageviews is not human traffic

Server-side analytics counts every request to the site, including search-engine crawlers, AI scrapers, uptime monitors and bots. Google Search Console counts only real people. The two disagree in a specific, diagnostic way: pageview data shows the United States at 48.8% and India at 7.4%, while verified search clicks show India at 64% and the US at 16%. The prominence of Singapore, Germany and China — all major data-centre regions — points the same way. Treat 76,538 as total server requests, not as 76,538 human visits. Installing Google Analytics 4 alongside the current setup would establish a clean, bot-filtered baseline of real sessions.

2. Impressions fell 16% but clicks rose 3% — the trade is favourable

The site is appearing in fewer search results but earning more clicks from them, which lifts CTR. This pattern usually reflects the loss of low-relevance, long-tail impressions rather than a loss of ranking quality, and the click growth confirms that core rankings held. It is worth monitoring: if impressions fall again for a second consecutive period, indexation and content coverage should be reviewed.

3. Search visibility is brand-dependent; discovery is the growth gap

Around 92% of clicks from the top queries come from people who already know the Hope Trust name. The queries that would bring in new, unaware clients — "psychologist hyderabad" (1,208 impressions, 9 clicks), "therapist near me" (1,229 impressions, 8 clicks) and "best psychologist in hyderabad" (580 impressions, 7 clicks) — together generate over 3,700 impressions but fewer than 40 clicks. The site is being shown for the right searches and is not being chosen, which is a function of ranking position (average 12.3, i.e. page two) rather than of demand.

4. The booking page is the strongest commercial signal in the data

/book-your-session/ is the third most-visited page (3,201 views) and its search clicks grew 19% — the largest gain of any core page. Traffic is reaching the point of enquiry. What is missing is measurement of what happens next: without conversion tracking, the number of these 3,201 visits that became a call, form submission or booking is unknown.

Methodology and notes

  • Sources. Two independent datasets: the hosting platform's server-side web analytics (pageviews, unique visitors, bandwidth, locations, top pages) and Google Search Console (clicks, impressions, CTR, position, queries, countries).
  • Date windows differ. Traffic data covers 30 days, 10 July to 9 August 2026. Search data covers Google's last-28-day window and lags by roughly two days, so the two are not directly comparable day for day.
  • Counting methods differ. Server-side analytics counts every request including bots and crawlers; Search Console counts only human clicks from Google Search. Figures from the two sources should not be added together.
  • Rounding. Google Search Console reports 1.9K clicks and 163K impressions; these are stated as 1,900 and 163,000 throughout. Percentage changes are as reported by Google against the immediately preceding equivalent period.
  • Partial day. 9 August 2026 is incomplete (data captured at 9:47 PM) and reads lower than a full day in the traffic trend.
  • Not yet measured. Traffic sources and channel split, bounce rate, session duration, device breakdown and conversions are not available from the current setup. Recommendations 1 and 2 address this.
