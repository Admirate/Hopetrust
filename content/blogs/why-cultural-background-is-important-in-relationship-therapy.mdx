Relationships, in our globalized world often bring together individuals from diverse cultural backgrounds. These variations can lead to misunderstandings, but they also hold the potential for enriching our understanding of the world.

Seeking therapy can help couples navigate these differences, but it must be done with cultural sensitivity. Cultural backgrounds shape our views on relationships, communication, conflict resolution, and emotional expression. Ignoring these elements in therapy could lead to inadequate solutions or even exacerbate the issues.

#### Why Cultural Backgrounds Count in Therapy

From the values we live by to how we show love and manage conflict, culture shapes all elements of our lives. (https://hopetrustindia.com/therapist-list/). Here are some important ways in which relationship therapy is significantly influenced by cultural background:

**_Styles of Communication_**

Various cultures have different means of expression. While some might prioritise indirect or nonverbal communication, others value direct, forceful communication. For instance, whereas in many Asian cultures, nonverbal signals and implied understanding hold more weight, in Western cultures, open conversations and vocal affirmations are often encouraged.

For instance, Priya and Arjun, a South Asian couple, communicate in their unique ways. Arjun, raised in India, tends to avoid confrontation out of respect and harmony; Priya, who grew up in the United States, values open discussions and direct communication. Their therapist guides them in finding a middle ground that fits their cultural points of view and helps them comprehend their different approaches.

**_Views on Gender Roles_**

Cultural backgrounds help to define ideas on gender roles in relationships. In some societies, traditional roles are underlined in which women are seen as carers and men as main providers. In others, one expects equality in duties. Therapy can help couples negotiate conflicting ideas about gender roles in a way that honours both of their cultural values and promotes mutual understanding.

For example, Raj expects Neha to give home chores top priority while he concentrates on work, as his parents did. Neha, on the other hand, was reared with more egalitarian values and looks for a relationship, whereby housework is divided. Their therapist guides them in discovering these strongly ingrained ideas and (https://hopetrustindia.com/blog/our-marriage-counsellors-share-5-truths-about-love/).

**_Family Participation_**

In many societies, relationships revolve around family to a significant extent. For example, collectivist societies—such as those in Latin America, Africa, and portions of Asia—often stress extended family participation in decision-making. On the other hand, individualistic societies might prioritise the couple’s autonomy. Relationship counselling has to consider these dynamics to assist couples in juggling their cultural expectations with their personal boundaries.

For instance, the parents of the newly married South Asian couple Ananya and Vikram pressure them since they expect Ananya to live with them and follow conventional daughter-in-law duties. They value independence, which Ananya finds excessive. Their therapist guides them in setting reasonable limits while honouring family customs.

**_Conflict Resolution Strategies_**

People’s handling of problems reflects a great deal of their cultural background. For instance, some societies support harmony and the avoidance of confrontation, while others encourage honest confrontation and problem-solving conversations. (https://hopetrustindia.com/blog/navigating-relationship-challenges-how-couples-therapy-can-strengthen-your-bond/). It’s important to understand these cultural differences to guide couples effectively in resolving conflicts.

For example, Meera prefers to confront problems head-on, while Rohan tends to withdraw and avoid confrontation since he was taught that conflicts should not be expressed. Their therapist guides them towards conflict-resolution strategies that honour their cultural influences.

**_Emotional Expression and Mental Health Stigma_**

Furthermore, cultural background influences how emotions are expressed and how mental health is seen. While some societies see expressing emotions as a sign of weakness, others promote emotional openness. Moreover, in some societies, getting therapy could be frowned upon. (https://hopetrustindia.com/therapist-list/).

As an illustration, Dev, from a South Asian background, struggles to communicate his feelings because he was taught to view sensitivity as a flaw. His emotional distance irritates his friend Aisha, who comes from a culture that promotes emotional openness. As Aisha gains more open communication, his therapist guides him to see that expressing emotions does not equate to weakness and advises patience. This example highlights how cultural background can significantly influence emotional expression and mental health stigma in a relationship.

#### What a Culturally Competent Therapist Does

Relationship counselling can benefit much from the cultural competency of a therapist. A therapist of such nature:

-   Acknowledges cultural variations without presumptions
-   Tries to know every partner’s background and values
-   Asks open-ended questions
-   Avoids cultural bias and values many points of view
-   Incorporates culturally relevant techniques for communication and conflict resolution
-   Encourages mutual respect among couples for their different backgrounds.
