We have all experienced that sinking feeling in our stomach when we walk into a counsellor’s room for the first time and realise that this will not be the person who can help us get better.

That’s why we want to share our tips for finding the right therapist:

## Ask your friends and family if they have used a therapist

Ask your friends and family if they have used a therapist. Asking for referrals is an excellent way to find the right person, as it will allow you to see what kind of experience others have had with that particular therapist.

If someone has used a specific therapist in the past, they may be more likely to recommend their services over other options available. Sometimes, your friend or family may recommend an ([Hope Trust](/)) – psychologists and psychiatrists, because you may need both for assessments, medication prescriptions, and psychotherapy.

Their recommendations might help guide your search process (e.g., “I’ve heard great things about this particular person!”). The more people willing to speak up about their experiences with different therapists, the better chance that one of those recommendations could prove helpful in finding just what you need!

## Ask your doctor for recommendations

Ask your doctor or other health provider for recommendations. They can tell you if they have referred their clients to a therapist. You can also search online to find a suitable one.

-   Research online to find the right therapist
-   Look for a therapist who has experience working with people like you
-   Look for a therapist who has received accreditation and is licensed by a reputable organization, such as the Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI).

## Tips on finding the right therapist

You might have questions about how to find the right therapist for you. Here are some tips about how to locate the most suitable counsellor:

_Look for a therapist who has experience working with people like you._ If they don’t have enough training or experience, they will likely be unable to help you on your ([What is the secret of self-discovery?](/blogs/what-is-the-secret-of-self-discovery/)) and growth.

_Find someone who is approachable and friendly._ This person should be willing to listen without judgment and provide helpful advice whenever needed (not just when requested). They should also be willing to answer any questions or concerns during sessions—this can happen even if those questions seem silly at first glance!

_Make sure the person is open about discussing personal issues_ like ([Should You Tell Your Friends About Your Depression?](/blogs/should-you-tell-your-friends-about-your-depression/)), anxiety, or relationship issues. Both parties understand each other’s needs before beginning treatment sessions together; this will help ensure you get what you need out of therapy sessions without worrying about sharing too much information early on in your relationship with each other.

## Finding a good therapist is always a good idea during tough times

Therapists are trained to help people with problems and have experience in many different areas of ([How to Maintain Positive Mental Health](/blogs/how-to-maintain-positive-mental-health/)). They are also trained to listen and understand you as an individual, which means they can help you find the right path for your life or family.

## Online or in person—which is best for you?

Online therapy provides a convenient, accessible, and affordable way to receive treatment, which is particularly beneficial in today’s fast-paced and digitally connected world.

_Online therapy_

There are several advantages to ([Does Online Mental Health Therapy Work for You?](/blogs/does-online-mental-health-therapy-work-for-you/)):

_Convenience:_ Online therapy can be accessed from anywhere with an internet connection, allowing you to receive treatment from the comfort of your home or any location of your choice. You don’t have to travel, so you save time. It’s also easier to receive therapy if you have a busy schedule or mobility issues.

_Accessibility:_ Online therapy is more accessible if you live in remote areas or have difficulty finding a therapist specializing in your needs.

_Affordability:_ Online therapy can be more affordable than traditional in-person therapy as it eliminates the cost of travel.

_Anonymity:_ Online therapy provides greater anonymity and privacy if you feel uncomfortable or stigmatized by seeking in-person treatment.

_Flexibility:_ Online therapy offers greater flexibility, as appointments can be scheduled outside regular business hours. This can make it easier to fit therapy into your busy schedule.

_In-person therapy_

In-clinic therapy gives a personal and tailored approach to treatment that you may prefer. It can also offer a more controlled environment and enhanced confidentiality.

While online therapy offers many benefits, ([How to Make the Most Out of Your Teletherapy](/blogs/how-to-make-the-most-out-of-your-teletherapy/)) also has several advantages, such as:

_Personal Connection_: In-person therapy allows for a personal connection between the therapist and you, which can help build trust and rapport. This connection is vital if you prefer face-to-face interaction.

_Nonverbal Communication:_ In-person therapy enables the therapist to observe and interpret nonverbal communication, such as ([First Impressions Last: How to Make a Good One](/blogs/first-impressions-last-how-to-make-a-good-one/)) and voice tone. This can provide valuable insights into the client’s emotions and thoughts, which your therapist may not notice in online therapy.

_Reduced Distractions:_ In-person therapy allows for a more controlled environment with fewer distractions from technology or other interruptions. This helps you stay focused on your therapy session and get the most out of it.

_Tailored Interventions:_ In-person therapy allows for tailored interventions that may not be possible in online therapy, such as exposure therapy, which involves gradually exposing a person to their fears or triggers in a controlled environment.

_Enhanced Confidentiality_: In-person therapy may offer greater confidentiality if you are concerned about privacy. This can be particularly important if you are uncomfortable discussing sensitive or personal issues online.
