## What is Cocaine?

Cocaine is a potent stimulant derived from the leaves of the coca plant native to South America.

Cocaine is also known as _coke, flake, snow, crack, white powder, and blow_

Cocaine impacts the neuropathways in the brain and makes the addict feel highly energetic, talkative, confident and euphoric. Addiction to this drug develops rapidly, even after using it only a few times.

## What is Cocaine Addiction?

Cocaine is one of the most addictive drugs and causes numerous overdoses and hospital admissions worldwide. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine) addiction has severe and permanent effects on the user and those around them.

Cocaine is consumed in various ways: snorting, inhaling through the nose or injecting into a vein. It can also be injected via the genital or rectal routes. It is also smoked after being processed into a form called crack cocaine.

## What are the effects of cocaine?

Cocaine boosts dopamine’s (a naturally occurring neurotransmitter in the brain) activity, which causes the addict to experience feelings of pleasure and euphoria because cocaine prevents dopamine, serotonin, and other neurotransmitters from being taken into the nerve cells. These neurotransmitters accumulate and stimulate the surrounding nerve cells, triggering an acute pleasure sensation.

Though some people claim that cocaine helps them to think more clearly and makes them more active, sustained use leads to intense craving and dysfunctional behaviour.

An addiction can be physical, meaning your body craves the drug. It can also be psychological, meaning the person desperately desires the drug’s effects. This effect lasts for about an hour, and the user will crave to use it again to maintain the high and prevent withdrawal symptoms.

Continued use of cocaine leads to a higher tolerance for the drug – the individual needs more cocaine to feel the same effects. This leads to using more significant amounts.

The psychological impact of cocaine addiction includes:

-   Paranoia and panic
-   Hallucinations
-   Aggressive behaviour
-   Irritability and anxiety
-   Depression
-   Impaired judgment
-   Dysfunctional behaviours

Physical effects of cocaine addiction may include:

-   Weight loss
-   Nausea
-   Abdominal pain and headaches
-   Chest pain
-   Increased and irregular heartbeat (arrhythmia)
-   Heart attack
-   Seizure
-   Stroke

Cocaine addiction is also associated with several severe medical conditions, including bowel gangrene, respiratory problems and hepatitis.

## What is the cause of cocaine addiction?

Cocaine impacts neurological systems in the brain. Repetitive use drives the person to severe addiction.

The neuroadaptation of the brain function increases dosage due to tolerance, impaired judgment, and craving, leading to cocaine addiction, which becomes hard to quit without professional intervention.

## Who is at risk for cocaine addiction?

Anyone who experiments even once with cocaine is at risk of becoming addicted. Other factors that increase the risk of cocaine addiction are:

-   Family history of drug dependence
-   Addiction to alcohol or other drugs
-   Mental disorders, such as (https://hopetrustindia.com/blog/the-different-types-of-bipolar-disorders/) or depression

## What are the symptoms of cocaine addiction?

Symptoms may include some or all of the following:

-   Continued use of the drug despite problems caused by the usage
-   tolerance for the drug – requiring a greater quantity to achieve the same high
-   Not able to stop or reduce the use of the drug
-   Withdrawal symptoms when usage stops
-   A negative impact on quality of life – health, relationships, education, and employment
-   Spending excessive time and money looking for and using cocaine
-   psychosis, hallucinations, fears
-   Anxiety and irritability
-   Lying and stealing to maintain the habit

## What is cocaine withdrawal?

When an addict stops using cocaine, they undergo withdrawal, which can be a challenging experience. Intense cravings and uncomfortable side effects accompany it. Withdrawal symbols include:

-   Tiredness
-   Paranoia
-   Anxiety
-   Agitation
-   Sleeplessness
-   Depression
-   Body aches

A supportive environment with medical supervision is essential at this stage.

## What are the treatment options for cocaine addiction?

Cocaine addiction treatment can be a challenging and complex process. The negative impact on all areas of the addict’s life has to be addressed – physical, psychological, social, emotional and familial. A holistic approach is the most effective to prevent relapse.

Recovery from cocaine addiction is a complex and gradual process, requiring a multi-dimensional approach. It is also a highly relapse-prone disease. If not treated, it destroys families and kills the addict.
